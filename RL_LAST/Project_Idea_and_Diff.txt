# 🎵 Project Overview: EEG-Based Music Emotion Management using Reinforcement Learning

## 🌟 The Core Idea
This project aims to bridge the gap between affective computing and therapeutic music intervention. The goal is to develop an intelligent Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent that can "steer" a listener's emotional state toward a target positive emotion (e.g., "Happy") by selecting an optimal sequence of music clips (a personalized playlist). The agent uses real-time physiological feedback in the form of EEG (Electroencephalogram) signals to understand the listener's emotional response and adjust its strategy accordingly.

---

## 🔬 What We Are Doing Differently (The "Diff")

Our work extends the baseline research (Dutta et al., 2020) in several critical ways to improve robustness, stability, and scientific depth:

### 1. Algorithmic Diversity & Comparison
- **Baseline**: Used only standard Tabular Q-Learning (Off-Policy).
- **Our Approach**: We implement and compare **four** distinct RL algorithms to find the most effective strategy for human emotional transitions:
    - **Q-Learning**: The off-policy baseline.
    - **SARSA**: An on-policy approach that learns from the actual actions taken, potentially leading to safer/smoother emotional transitions.
    - **Double Q-Learning**: Addresses the "Maximization Bias" (overestimation of Q-values) inherent in standard Q-learning, leading to more accurate value estimates.
    - **Double SARSA**: A hybrid approach combining the stability of Double learning with the on-policy nature of SARSA.

### 2. Rigorous Performance Evaluation
- We implemented a **two-tier convergence criterion** to measure success more precisely:
    - **Strict Convergence**: Final angular error < 20°.
    - **Relaxed Convergence**: Final angular error < 30°.
- This provides a more granular understanding of how close the agent gets to the target emotion compared to the simple "success/failure" metrics in previous work.

### 3. Systematic Hyperparameter Optimization
- Instead of using fixed values, we implemented a formal **Grid Search with 10-fold Cross-Validation** to find the optimal Learning Rate (α), Discount Factor (γ), and Exploration Rate (ε) for each specific subject.

### 4. Advanced Reward Shaping & Ablation Study
- We performed a detailed **Ablation Study** on the Reward Shaping function (λ = -100). By running experiments both *with* and *without* shaping, we scientifically quantified exactly how much the shaping helps the agent avoid local minima (emotional "stuck" states).

### 5. Multi-Dataset Extensibility
- While the baseline focused on the **DEAP dataset**, our framework is architected to be modular and is already being tested on the **DENSE dataset**, allowing for cross-dataset validation of the music-emotion model.

---

## 🛠️ Technical Implementation
- **State Space**: The Geneva Emotion Wheel (GEW) discretized into a 20x20 grid (400 states).
- **Action Space**: A library of 40 music clips per subject, each with unique Valence-Arousal ratings.
- **Cross-Validation**: Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) across 32 subjects to ensure the model generalizes to new listeners.
- **Stack**: Python, NumPy, Pandas, Matplotlib for analytics and visualization.

---

## 📈 Summary of Results
Our implementation of Double Q-Learning and Double SARSA showed more stable convergence patterns and successfully guided a majority of subjects (19/32) to the target state with a mean angular error significantly lower than the baseline thresholds. The comparison charts (saved in `results_compare/`) provide visual proof of the efficiency gains achieved by our algorithmic extensions.
